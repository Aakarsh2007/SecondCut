# SecondCut competitive review — 29 October 2026

This is a focused public-source review, not exhaustive novelty clearance. Competitor descriptions are self-reported capabilities; their software, customers and performance were not independently tested. No outreach was performed.

## Existing alternatives

| Alternative | What its public page supports | What SecondCut should demonstrate | What we cannot claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Unbuilt](https://unbuilt.co/shop/how-it-works) | Marketplace for unused design/building inventory, including builders, designers and manufacturers | A raw tabletop that can satisfy a smaller custom request after explicitly priced maker-approved cuts | That resale of cancelled inventory is new, or that we have a comparable buyer network |
| [Ghost](https://www.ghst.io/) | AI-supported surplus distribution, approved buyers/sellers and channel controls | One workshop's physical adaptation and original-deposit settlement, with exact approvals and provider evidence | Better liquidity, reach, price discovery or enterprise scale |
| [Inventory Matchmakers](https://www.inventorymatchmakers.com/for-brands) | Hands-on matching around inventory, price, location and reseller/channel fit; search-index content retrieved, direct page fetch failed | Repeatable dimensional/financial rejection and transaction coordination on a specific custom order | That contextual matching is unique, or that our small synthetic request set represents real demand |
| [Chairish](https://support.chairish.com/hc/en-us/articles/44166765189137-Managing-Returns-Cancellations) | Existing furniture resale transaction/cancellation context | Recovery while a custom piece remains adaptable, with original and replacement buyer obligations separately tracked | That established marketplaces lack all similar internal functionality |

These services may be better for finding ready buyers or selling finished goods. SecondCut's proposed entry point is the workshop decision before irreversible completion. It could complement a resale channel; a new broad marketplace is unnecessary for this MVP.

Additional direct evidence makes the novelty boundary narrower: [9th Street Designer Clearance](https://www.9thstreetdesignerclearance.com/our-story) explicitly describes a business selling cancelled special orders from designers/factories. [Forest Contract's indexed clearance page](https://restorefc.k2l.co.uk/clearance-furniture/) describes cancelled inventory and some raw stock that can be upholstered or finished; direct retrieval failed. Reselling or customizing unfinished cancellation stock is therefore not an exclusive invention. The differentiating hypothesis is the integrated decision, exact-term approval and verified original/replacement settlement workflow.

[Vermont Woods Studios](https://vermontwoodsstudios.com/policies/refund-policy) explains that customer-specific finished pieces are difficult to resell and wood purchasing/scheduling can begin promptly. This is current primary problem evidence, not a cancellation-rate estimate, evidence of demand for SecondCut, or a legal rule to apply elsewhere.

## Positioning and demonstration decisions

**SecondCut turns a cancelled custom order into a reviewed adaptation and a verified replacement-sale/refund workflow.**

1. Show a non-exact request: 1800 × 800 mm raw stock, 1600 × 700 mm requested desk. Explain both supported cuts, quotes and limits. Do not claim structural safety is AI-certified.
2. Show a refusal beside success: an oversized request, unsupported finish change, low budget or expired consent remains rejected. This makes intelligence and boundaries visible.
3. Compare identical scenarios using an explicitly defined exact-specification baseline. Report modeled feasibility and cost results, not competitor accuracy, real conversion or time saved.
4. Use source-cited AI extraction for unstructured requirements. Explain that deterministic rules, current approvals and PayPal readback govern commitments. Do not describe bounded rules-based counteroffers as autonomous negotiation.
5. Demonstrate the already completed joined sandbox journey: three accounts, original deposit, distinct replacement payer, capture, original partial refund and linked signed events. Those accounts belong to one tester; no independent buyer demand is implied.
6. Keep the walkthrough short. Public source must run cleanly. Show one success and one blocked case, rather than add industries or agents for appearance.

## Remaining competitive risks

- Buyer availability is still unproven. No marketplace or public anecdote creates a replacement customer for a particular workpiece.
- Maker cost/constraint inputs are synthetic. A credible commercial claim needs permissioned real records and maker validation.
- No controlled customer study or direct competitor performance experiment exists. We cannot claim to beat every competitor or guarantee a win.
- The strongest feasible submission now is a narrow, transparent, technically complete prototype plus reproducible evidence and clear limits.
