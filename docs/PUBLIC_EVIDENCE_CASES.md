# Public-source case review

Reviewed 29 October 2026. Public sources only; no interviews, outreach, purchases or private records. These are contextual observations, not customers of SecondCut. Listing publication is not discovery consent for a replacement-buyer request. No source was imported as a payable case.

## P1 — cancellation after material investment

Source: [WOODWEB, Managing a Customer Cancellation](https://woodweb.com/knowledge_base/Managing_a_Customer_Cancellation.html), 21 April 2011; original question and original author's follow-up.

Supported facts: a kitchen order of approximately USD 20,000; cut sheets prepared; materials delivered; customer requested cancellation; original author reports a 50% deposit and a signed agreement.

Unknown: actual incurred cost, remaining labour, material/stock dimensions, cancellation outcome, refundable balance and another buyer's requirements. Replies' suggested material costs or settlement amounts are opinions, not established facts. No legal entitlement is inferred.

SecondCut decision: **OUTSIDE CURRENT PRODUCT SCOPE / NOT ACTIONABLE**. A kitchen cannot be represented as the supported raw tabletop workflow. The report supports existence of stranded investment, not profitable desk adaptation or current demand.

## P2 — an actual advertised cancelled table order

Source: [RCC Furniture, table listing 654](https://www.rcc-furniture.com/product-page/in-stock-180x90x75-reclaimed-industrial-straight-leg-table-654). Search-index result retrieved; direct page retrieval failed. The title identifies a sold 180 × 90 × 75 table and the indexed description identifies it as new cancelled-order stock. The listing has previously indexed inconsistent dimension text, so title values are not accepted as verified manufacturing measurements.

Unknown: current availability, actual final measurements, material species/finish, permitted cuts, incurred/remaining costs, original payment/refund obligations and an opt-in replacement request. A sale label is not independent proof of a completed transaction, its price or profit.

SecondCut decision: **UNAVAILABLE / NEEDS MAKER EVIDENCE**. Do not turn a sold listing into available stock, derive production cost from an advertised price, convert GBP to USD silently or treat a seller's listing as a buyer's request. This supports cancellation resale as an existing practice, not the proposed adaptation's safety or profitability.

## P3 — a maker's published explanation of resale difficulty

Source: [Vermont Woods Studios refund policy](https://vermontwoodsstudios.com/policies/refund-policy), directly retrieved.

Supported facts: the business describes prompt wood purchasing/scheduling and difficulty reselling furniture produced for an individual customer. It also describes storage charges under specified conditions.

Unknown: any particular cancelled order's costs, measurements, legal obligations or buyer match. The policy is specific to that business and is not a universal rule.

SecondCut decision: **PROBLEM CONTEXT ONLY**. Do not create a transaction or a quote from a policy page. Storage timing is a reason to collect real carrying costs; the published fee must not be imposed on unrelated merchants or treated as a universal financial assumption.

## P4 — established cancellation-clearance channels

Sources: [9th Street Designer Clearance](https://www.9thstreetdesignerclearance.com/our-story) and [Joseph James Furniture Outlet](https://josephjamesfurnitureoutlet.com/pages/how-it-works), directly retrieved.

Supported facts: these businesses describe sourcing cancelled orders among the inventory they resell. This confirms existing alternatives and potential benchmark channels.

Unknown: their realized recovery margins, conversion rates, customer acquisition costs, cancellation frequency, internal matching algorithms or willingness to integrate SecondCut.

SecondCut decision: **ALTERNATIVE CHANNEL, NOT A LIVE INTEGRATION**. A supported outlet quote could eventually inform the cancellation baseline. A marketing discount claim is not a maker's net salvage quote. We did not contact or integrate either business.

## What the review establishes

Public evidence supports that cancelled custom/special orders and resale difficulty exist, and that merchants already use clearance channels. It does not establish demand for SecondCut, a current compatible buyer, an inspected physical adaptation, real manufacturing economics or measured superiority over those channels.

The strongest public-source-only demonstration combines these cited problem/alternative observations with the actual sandbox financial journey and clearly synthetic planner scenarios. The software should refuse incomplete public records rather than invent missing inputs to produce a profitable match.

## Data-use boundary

Retain links, retrieval limitations and short factual paraphrases. Do not publish personal customer identities, scrape contacts, send messages, claim partnerships or label the source businesses as SecondCut customers. No testimonial, interview or real-world recovery is implied. See the [validation playbook](../app/docs/VALIDATION_PLAYBOOK.md) for the evidence still needed before a commercial pilot.
